Get Atrium

Terms, in plain words.

Atrium is free, open-source software, made by one person and shared under the MIT License. There's no company behind it and nothing to sign up for. This is what that means for you.

Updated November 18, 2026

The one condition: the copyright notice and the license text stay with every copy or substantial part of it. The license file is the legal text; this page only explains it.

No warranty

Atrium comes as is. Nobody promises that it works, that it suits what you want it for, or that it won't break something, and nobody who made it is liable for any harm that comes from using it, from lost work to an unexpected bill. Keep backups of what matters.

Its checks and reviews are evidence, not proof. The loop docs list what they can't catch.

What you're responsible for

  • What you approve. Agents draft and you decide. Anything you approve, push, merge or publish is your own work, so read the diff first.
  • The rules you turn on. A rule approves a repeated kind of result for you: only docs-only changes, or text answers that keep no files. It can never approve a push, a merge to main, a delete, secrets or a new repo, but what it does approve is still your call.
  • Where it works. You choose the repos and folders Atrium and its agents work in, and the accounts the agent CLIs and GitHub are signed in to.

The agents' own terms and costs

The agents Atrium runs are other companies' products, used through your own accounts and under their terms. Their usage limits and any charges are between you and them. The costs Atrium shows are estimates at list prices, not bills. One task can use more than one of them: by default a code task's review goes to another maker's CLI when one is installed, and briefs and walkthroughs run on Claude. The privacy page says which step goes where.

  • Claude Code: Anthropic's consumer terms, or its commercial terms for a work account.
  • Codex: OpenAI's terms of use.
  • Gemini through Antigravity: Google's terms of service and Antigravity's additional terms.
  • GitHub, for clones, pushes and pull requests: GitHub's terms of service.
